This is the most common question we get. The short answer is: **Solid wood is a living material, and it moves.**

Understanding *why* it moves is the key to preventing damage.

## 1. Wood Breathes (Even After Cutting)
Think of wood like a bundle of microscopic straws. When the tree is alive, these straws transport water. When cut and dried, these straws stay open.
*   **High Humidity (Summer):** The straws absorb moisture from the air. The wood **expands**.
*   **Low Humidity (Winter):** The straws release moisture. The wood **shrinks**.

This movement is natural. A 40-inch wide table might actually be 40.25" in July and 39.75" in February.

## 2. When Does It Crack?
Cracking happens when the wood tries to shrink, but something stops it.
*   **Bad Design:** If a table base is screwed directly into the wood without "slotted holes" (which allow the screw to slide as the wood moves), the wood will pull itself apart. *Note: Urbis Concepts bases allow for this movement.*
*   **Extreme Dryness:** If humidity drops below 30% (common in Canadian winters), the wood shrinks so aggressively that its own fibers snap.

## 3. How We Prevent It (The Urbis Way)
We take three critical steps before the table ever reaches your home:
1.  **Proper Kiln Drying:** We dry slabs to 8-10% moisture content. This removes the bulk of the water so the wood is stable *before* finish.
2.  **Epoxy Filling:** Any natural knots or existing voids are filled with specialized epoxy, stabilizing weak points.
3.  **C-Channel Reinforcement:** Large slabs are routed underneath and fitted with steel C-Channels. These keep the slab flat while still allowing it to expand/contract sideways.

## 4. How You Prevent It (Your Job)
You have one responsibility: **Humidity Control.**
*   Aim for **40–45% humidity** in your home.
*   Don't place the table directly over a heating vent.
*   Don't place the table in direct, baking sunlight all day.

If a crack does appear, don't panic. Solid wood is repairable. A butterfly key (dovetail key) or epoxy fill can fix it, often adding even more character to the piece.
